## Design Context

### Users
The owner, using a simple practical map to find movie theaters in Bali and compare where to go. The creator confirmed that this is mainly for their own use.

### Brand Personality
Clear, calm, useful. Help people orient themselves and reach a theater's schedule quickly.

### Aesthetic Direction
The creator's reference is their Australia 462 specified-work map: light basemap, compact left side panel, direct search and location selection. Keep that visual structure, adapting the content to cinemas. Use restrained navy and amber accents with theater-chain colors only where meaningful.

### Design Principles
- The map and theater list are the first screen.
- Every pin corresponds to a documented cinema or its hosting mall.
- Names, addresses, and schedule links matter more than decoration.
- Search and selection work with a keyboard and touch.
- Explain the listing's coverage and data date without implying live ticket availability.
- On phones, use one continuous page scroll instead of a fixed map/list split. The creator found the split confusing.
- Keep alerts, owner refresh, pricing/format explanations and about information in one Settings & help menu beside the title; they must not interrupt the movie controls and results.
- Use one full-width Cinema format dropdown above the movie picker. The creator found separate Movie experience and Studio format controls redundant, experience sounded like a skill level, and individual format buttons added visual noise.
- Keep language filtering in a compact Spoken language dropdown and label missing metadata Unknown. Keep dubbing/subtitle caveats in Settings → About this map; the creator found a permanent warning beside the filter noisy.
- Group standard seating as Regular or Deluxe. Explain Executive alongside Premiere, VIP and IMAX in Settings; do not silently include Executive in a filter explicitly named Premiere, VIP or IMAX. Link directly to seating photos from the format names, without inline photos in Settings.
